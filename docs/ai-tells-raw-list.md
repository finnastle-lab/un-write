# AI writing tells — raw list

A flat reference of the tells, pulled from the Tekspace catalogue, the 2026 editing pass, and the walk memos. No argument, no framing. Grouped by type. Each is a flag to look harder at a sentence, not a rule.

## Punctuation and typography
- Em dashes and en dashes (hyphens only)
- Exclamation marks
- Capitalising For Emphasis
- Title case section headings mid-article
- Bold on a phrase every second sentence
- Curly quotes and stray markdown artefacts pasted through

## Structural / rhythm
- Sentences marching in formation (uniform 15-20 word length across a paragraph)
- Staccato cadence (three or more short sentences in a row)
- All-fragment lists
- Setup-then-list openers ("Three things surprised me...")
- Negation-fragment-affirmation ("Not a discovery project. A formalisation project.")
- Build-and-crescendo every few paragraphs; breathless transitions
- Punchy two-word closers ("Use it." "Read it.")
- Rule of three as a crutch (tripled adjectives, tripled clauses, three-item lists everywhere)
- Arguments that teleport (missing logical bridge between sentences)
- Internal references too tidy (opening line called back in closing line; threads resolving too neatly)
- Faux balance (both sides acknowledged, neither weighed, no conclusion drawn)
- False ranges ("from phishing to BEC" — two adjacent things posing as a spectrum)

## Pseudo-structure / manufactured weight
- Pseudo-cleft emphasis ("What matters here is...", "What X actually does is...") — reshape of a plain claim
- Argument signposting ("the part worth pausing on", "the finding underneath the headline")
- Structure narration ("Taking those in order", "Answering the first part first")
- Sound-matched negation pairs ("causal, not coincidental" — second half chosen for phonetics)
- Superficial analysis (an -ing clause bolted on: "...reflecting the growing importance of...", "...highlighting the need for...")

## Padding / setup without payoff
- Announcing the good part ("here's the kicker", "here's what most people miss")
- Announcing honesty ("honest answer", "the truth is", "let me be blunt", "here's the thing", "worth noting")
- Setup without delivery (the blunt promise, then nothing blunt)
- Therapist mode ("you're not alone in this", "that's completely understandable", "and that's okay")
- Questions asking permission to continue ("Want to go deeper?", "Sound familiar?")
- Engagement-farming closers ("What do you think?", "DM me for more")
- True statements that teach nothing ("Consistency matters.", "Visibility is important.")
- Summarising conclusions ("in summary", "to recap", "the bottom line is")
- Meta-narration ("that last point shaped how we wrote this")

## Contrast-reframe family
- "It's not X, it's Y"
- "Not just X, but Y"
- "Less X, more Y"
- "X is table stakes, Y is the real question"
- "The question isn't X, it's Y"

## Phrase-level / transitions / hedging
- Stiff transitions (furthermore, moreover, in addition, that said) — one per piece max
- Hedge stacking ("may potentially", "could arguably", "often tends to")
- Complex prepositional clichés ("taking into account", "with regard to", "in terms of", "when it comes to")
- Vague attributions ("industry experts say", "research suggests", "it's widely reported")
- Propping intensifiers ("three genuinely separate...", "genuinely", "really" as filler)
- Metaphors that almost land (technically accurate, emotionally off)

## Vocabulary watchlist (flag, don't auto-replace)
quietly · seamless · delve · navigate (figurative) · robust · comprehensive · cutting-edge · game-changer · leverage · unlock · harness · embark · fast-paced · elevate · foster · underscore · testament · stands as · plays a vital role · watershed moment · rich (figurative) · built different · paradigm shift · next-generation · industry-leading · best-of-breed

Check-the-sentence group (higher false-positive rate): shift · matters · shape · land (figurative) · actually · real (as intensifier) · hold (figurative) · pull (as noun) · compound (as verb) · signal (as verb) · the work

## Vendor / cliché openers (domain-flavoured but same species)
- "In an era of..." / "In today's threat landscape..." / "In today's digital landscape..."
- "It's not a matter of if, but when"
- "Sophisticated threat actors"
- "Don't be the next victim..."
- Military metaphors as decoration ("cyber warriors")
- FUD lines ("hackers are coming for your business")
- Generic openers ("Exciting news...", "It's important to note that...")

## The working-relationship tells (not on the public lists yet)
- Prompt-contaminated vocabulary — the model pulls a task/technical word (a file name, a variable) into a spoken sentence where a person wouldn't
- Performed warmth with no one to perform for — considerate-colleague register in a context where both parties know it's an AI and there's no relationship to protect
- Trait-caricature — fed your own writing, it latches onto one or two surface features and cranks them past where you'd ever take them
- The assistant house style — over-helpful, over-thorough, confident faintly-corporate vernacular that works while being obviously not-you

## Sourcing (credibility tells, not just style)
- Statistics with no attribution, or attributed to a body that wouldn't publish that figure
- Citations that look right and don't resolve
- Global figures presented as local

## Do NOT flag (house-style false positives, per Tekspace)
"earn" as a verb · Oxford comma · AU/British English · "signal" where it means what it says · structured numbered/bulleted lists (uniform bullets are the tell, not lists themselves) · second-person "you" · natural contractions

## Note on decay
Vocabulary tells turn over roughly every model release (currently every few months). Structural tells are far more durable. Weight structure higher than vocabulary in any tool built on this list.
